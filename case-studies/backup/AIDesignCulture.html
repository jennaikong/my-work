Building AI Design Culture

How I shaped the way a healthcare design org thinks about, builds with, and grows through AI.

Overview

The Work Behind the Work

The Context

As AI reshaped what product teams could build, design needed to evolve too — not just in output, but in mindset, process, and culture. I saw a gap and stepped in to close it.

My Role

Beyond my core product work, I took on org-wide design leadership: running hackathons, establishing AI design principles, and bringing external knowledge back in through hands-on internal workshops.

The Impact

A design team that's more confident, more creative, and better equipped to lead AI product decisions — not just react to them.

The Challenge

A team moving fast without a map

As GenAI took off in early 2023, product teams at Included Health moved quickly — but without a shared foundation. AI features were getting added reactively, driven more by technology push than user need. The result was a fragmented experience and a design team that felt underprepared to lead.

A survey I ran across our design org surfaced the core gap: designers weren't lacking talent or ambition — they lacked a framework. They needed structure for how to think about AI, evaluate its fit, and advocate for users in rooms where engineers and PMs were setting the agenda.

The opportunity wasn't just to design better AI features. It was to build a culture where designers could lead AI conversations with confidence — and bring the rest of the org with them.

The Approach

Three ways I shaped the culture

Rather than waiting for culture to change on its own, I created the conditions for it — through structured innovation, principled design standards, and continuous learning brought back from the outside in.

The three pillars of this work:

  • Hackathons that brought engineering, product, and design together to build, experiment, and stretch creatively — turning cross-functional energy into shipped features
  • A set of AI design values, principles, and UI patterns that gave the team a shared language and a user-centered foundation for every AI experience we built
  • Internal workshops translating external certifications into practical, hands-on learning — making sure new knowledge didn't stop at me
AI UX design patterns framework
How It Came Together

Three pillars, in depth

01

Hackathons

I organized cross-functional hackathons for engineering, product, and design to experiment freely and build together. The format grew participation 10x compared to before I led the initiative — and created a culture where designers felt empowered to prototype ideas, not just polish them.

02

AI Design Principles

I established a set of AI design values, principles, and reusable UI patterns grounded in a user-centered process. These became the foundation for every AI experience we shipped — giving the broader team a shared standard to design from and evaluate against.

03

External Learning, Brought In

I earned certifications in Object-Oriented UX, communication strategy, and AI product design — and after each one, ran a practical internal workshop to share the frameworks hands-on. Learning didn't stop at me; it became a resource for the whole org.

04

Scaling the Mindset

The goal was never a single workshop or artifact — it was a shift in how the team approaches AI problems. By modeling the process, building reusable tools, and creating space for experimentation, I helped move the org from reactive AI adoption to intentional AI design.

Impact

Results that compounded across the org

50%

10×
Designer Participation

Designer involvement in hackathons grew tenfold after I took over leadership — a signal that the format finally felt relevant and empowering for the design discipline.

75%
